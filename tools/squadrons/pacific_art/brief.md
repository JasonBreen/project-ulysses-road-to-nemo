# Pacific squadron stories: original artwork brief

Planning source for the approved new OSE/YUK story components. This draft is not
part of the Osea 44 delivery. Eight original portraits, sixteen scene masters and
two independent loading masters are required. Root is coordinating the existing
art agent; the scripting owner has started no duplicate imagegen calls.

Use original realistic painted aviation editorial art, natural light/skin tones,
and restrained navy/cyan briefing structure. No text, numbers, signature,
watermark, yellow overlay, flag, logo, invented official emblem, recognizable
Earth landmark, national leader or established Ace Combat cameo. No strategic
weapon, destroyed named ship/city, compulsory victory, peace treaty or border.
Unverified scenery is illustrative authored fiction, not official geography.
Keep machinery conventional for 1991–2020 and avoid an identifiable aircraft
model unless separately approved. Parked machinery is background illustration,
not telemetry about an actual native formation. Base scenes are grounded; do
not depict airborne combat or a historical battle in their shared artwork.

Portrait masters should be separate files with their full source records and
stable character identity. Plain practical clothing, no rank/name patches. Frame
head/shoulders so hands, equipment and an injury are not permanently attached to
every later portrait. Ages below describe the initial 1991 act, not a claimed
official service biography. All eight people are new original supporting cast.

## Scene-slot contract

There are eight masters per country. Slots 1–5 serve the twenty core chapters;
slots 6/7/8 are distinct personal trusted/compromised/fractured ending artwork.
The new campaigns will have explicit per-campaign chapter/ending slot metadata.
The original six campaigns and their existing global slot/default rules remain
unchanged. No ending image assumes a national victory or peace.

later mapping change must be explicit in the story/art source ledger.

## Sixteen concrete scene masters

1. **OSE 1 — The first receiving shift.** An ordinary coastal aviation workshop
   at morning, seen from a doorway: two unmarked flight-suited people listening
   to a maintenance chief beside a closed conventional aircraft hangar. A modest
   control-room window in the background; everyday work and an unsettled first
   introduction. No airborne aircraft or ready-for-war formation. Shared by
   arrival, learning to accept correction, and the later return to an old routine.
2. **OSE 2 — The second frequency.** A small grounded control room at dusk with
   one controller viewed from behind, an unmarked headset, soft radar-like
   illumination, a second silent telephone and a blank paper map. An observer
   waits at the door rather than issuing a triumphant instruction. Supports
   uncertainty, listening and contrasting accounts in peace, war or external
   crisis; no readable tracks, specific enemy or tactical success.
3. **OSE 3 — The part that came back.** A maintenance bench in natural side light,
   with a carefully opened conventional aircraft component, two inspection tools,
   a plain cloth and a returned empty transport case. Two anonymous people are
   standing on different sides of the bench, faces not prominent. Emphasize the
   distance between an accepted signature and a repair somebody can actually use.
   No explosion, free new factory, visible battle damage or identified aircraft.
4. **OSE 4 — The chair kept free.** A quiet service break room adjoining the
   workshop: an empty chair, a closed first-aid kit, a plain flight helmet and a
   folded jacket beside a cup of tea. A colleague is visible through the doorway.
   No injured identifiable person, blood, hospital crisis or death implication.
   Supports the injury/rest/accountability thread and peaceful workshop variants
   without making a permanent bandage part of every later chapter.
5. **OSE 5 — An account carried home.** An unmarked airfield perimeter in soft
   overcast light, viewed from ground level. Two service personnel walk toward a
   small waiting civilian bus; a third remains by the workshop door with a closed
   folder. People are going to an ordinary next responsibility, not fleeing an
   asserted defeat. No surrender, foreign flags, rebuilt city or national result.
   Supports promises, family obligations and the final core-chapter reflection.
6. **OSE 6 — Shared debrief (trusted ending only).** Four original crew members,
   mostly from behind/three-quarter view, gathered around a small table in a
   practical training room with an open blank notebook and four cups. Warm late
   light, chairs close enough to suggest earned trust; nobody is saluting or
   celebrating a war victory. Use portrait references if faces are visible.
   The feeling is permission to tell the same difficult story together.
7. **OSE 7 — The protected version (compromised ending only).** Two separated
   pairs of service personnel in a modest administrative room divided by frosted
   glass, with a closed folder on a receiving desk. Their clothes link them to the
   original cast, but no national officeholder is present. Restrained natural
   light and careful distance; protective silence rather than espionage spectacle
   or imprisonment. No readable seal, badge, accusation or state verdict.
8. **OSE 8 — Different departures (fractured ending only).** A grounded service
   changing room near the end of a shift: four simple hooks, one open travel bag,
   a plain jacket left on a chair, and a doorway showing people leaving in
   different directions. No body, funeral or suggestion that a core person died.
   The fracture is separation and an unfinished conversation, with a little light
   still entering the doorway. No imposed defeat or occupation.

9. **YUK 1 — The names on the board.** A modest continental air-service workshop
   in pale morning light. A plain board carries blank paper slips without legible
   characters; a pilot and a technician examine the receiving arrangement while
   another person waits with a coat over an arm. A closed hangar beyond. No
   aircraft count, serviceability number, official unit crest or asserted force.
   Shared by joining the crew, learning who answers and revisiting that promise.
10. **YUK 2 — An answer that can be heard.** A quiet operations room with an
    anonymous controller seated beside a simple unmarked headset and two separate
    telephones. Another crew member listens from a chair, with a blank route sheet
    between them. Cold daylight balanced by a warm desk lamp. No readable enemy
    contact, launch command or specific war. Supports response-chain disagreement
    and a peaceful technical exercise with the same grounded composition.
11. **YUK 3 — Work before the return.** A maintenance bay with an opened panel on
    a conventional machine, a trolley of tools, a folded blank inspection sheet
    and two technicians viewed obliquely. Snow or rain outside is illustrative
    seasonal atmosphere, not a named canonical location. The machinery is being
    examined, not magically restored. No destroyed city, operational weapon,
    named airframe or claim of a delivered native production line.
12. **YUK 4 — The duty not taken.** A simple crew rest room with a closed first-aid
    box, a pair of gloves left on the table and an unused chair. One person waits
    near a window while another sets down an ordinary meal. Avoid visible core
    injuries or fixed medical status so the image also fits uninjured/late-entry
    branches. No death implication. Supports fatigue, injury care, refusal and
    the cost of treating a request as somebody else's available time.
13. **YUK 5 — What remains to be received.** A small workshop classroom after a
    lesson, with an anonymous older crew member placing a tool beside a blank
    notebook while a younger adult waits by a practical coat rack. No medal,
    official banner, readable text or declared national recovery. The mood is
    whether a useful practice can be handed on when a partner refuses, a war
    continues, or a previous promise remains unanswered.
14. **YUK 6 — A place for the next person (trusted ending only).** The four
    original core crew members, mostly from behind, showing a younger adult a
    simple workshop procedure around one bench. Four distinct silhouettes/clothes
    should follow the cast references if visible. Warm but restrained daylight;
    ordinary tools, no factory expansion, victory or state endorsement. The image
    resolves personal trust through teaching and the willingness to admit limits.
15. **YUK 7 — The signed silence (compromised ending only).** A receiving desk
    between two pairs of original crew members, a sealed unmarked envelope and
    an untouched cup. The room is functional and inhabited, not a prison or spy
    headquarters. Faces are restrained; any visible faces follow portrait
    references. The crew has preserved a version of its account at a personal
    cost. No peace treaty, coup, national betrayal or canonical cameo.
16. **YUK 8 — The board left behind (fractured ending only).** A service corridor
    with the original plain board now carrying a few empty clips, four hooks and
    a travel bag beside an open doorway. Two people are visible leaving in
    different directions, not arrested or killed. No readable roster or casualty
    list. The image suggests a crew dispersed through transfers and broken trust,
    with work continuing somewhere beyond the frame; no required national defeat.

## Two independent loading masters

- **OSE loading — The coast between shifts.** A wide cinematic view of an
  anonymous coastal aviation station at dawn: low hangars, a grounded conventional
  aircraft silhouette mostly under cover, a service vehicle, calm water beyond,
  and small original crew figures walking from a lit operations building. Navy
  and cyan mood with natural sunrise light; no yellow overlay. Leave a calm
  central/low-detail region suitable for the native loading text. No named city,
  recognizable Earth landmark, airborne battle, strategic spacecraft, flags,
  text or forced-war outcome. This is a separate original composition, not a
  widened event crop.
- **YUK loading — A receiving window in winter.** A wide cinematic view of an
  anonymous continental aviation maintenance station in restrained winter light:
  a warm open workshop, parked service equipment, a quiet rail/road receiving
  approach, a closed distant hangar and a few small original workers. Snow is
  illustrative scenery, not official mapped geography. Navy/cyan structural
  palette with natural warm workshop light and a calm text-safe region. No
  aircraft numbers, forced national recovery, giant weapon, battle, logos,
  flags, text or watermark. Independent framing rather than an event-image crop.
